What's borrowed from edh.dev, and what's new

      
The core idea is the same: one big 3D retro computer, scroll moves the camera into the screen, and the screen is the real content. Everything else gets turned up.

    

    

      

        
Borrowed

        
    
          
  • A single retro computer as the hero, built in Three.js (edh.dev uses a Commodore desktop)
    • 
          
  • Scroll moves the camera into the screen
    • 
          
  • Damped pointer parallax, and dragging to turn the desk
    • 
          
  • A working shell on the screen (edh.dev’s EDSH becomes jaysh)
    • 
          
  • A boot sequence as the loading moment
    • 
        

      

      

        
New for Jay

        
    
          
  • A flip-open 90s laptop. It starts shut with a glowing JAY logo. Scrolling lifts the lid, which boots it, and then the camera dives into the screen.
    • 
          
  • A full desktop OS on the screen, not just a shell: draggable windows, folders, apps
    • 
          
  • The screen is live DOM, so when you zoom in, the text is full size and fully clickable
    • 
          
  • Physical props do things. Floppies slide into the laptop’s side drive and open apps, the power button really shuts it down (and the lid closes when you step back), dog tags swing on their hook, the lamp sets the mood, and the 3D keys press when you type.
    • 
          
  • Full-screen when you’re in. The camera dives until the laptop’s screen covers the whole view, and then JAY-OS takes over the full window.
    • 
          
  • A 90s pixel-art Jay. Your photo is shrunk and dithered down to a 16-colour palette. It’s used in the About window and the polaroid on the wall.
    • 
          
  • A text-free first impression. Before the laptop opens there are no words on screen: just the room, a small animated scroll icon, and a Recruiter view button in the corner.
    • 
          
  • A living pixel-art Montréal as the desktop. The Grande Roue turns, cars cross the Jacques Cartier Bridge, the ferry sails past, snow falls, office lights flicker, and a raccoon patrols the Old Port. Click around to find secrets: the moon becomes a bagel, the Mont-Royal cross changes colour, the raccoon bolts. It shifts between night, golden hour and a snow day with the real time in Montréal.
    • 
          
  • Visitor Wall. Draw in Paint and pin it. Jay approves each drawing, then it shows up for everyone, on the corkboard in the room and in the Visitor Wall app. (UI preview here. The database comes with the real build.)
    • 
          
  • Games: BugHunt (bugs crawl over the desktop and you squash them), Snake that eats skills, and Paint that saves your art as a desktop icon
    • 
          
  • 13 achievements to reward exploring
    • 
          
  • Jay's world: a late-night Montréal dorm with snow and the Mont-Royal cross in the window, Montréal and Seoul clocks, a Marine Corps salute in the shell

  • Fixes edh.dev's gaps: recruiter view, tap support, a flat full-screen OS on phones, reduced-motion mode, and a fallback if 3D fails

How the real site would be built

      
This prototype is plain Three.js plus DOM. The production version keeps the same idea on your current stack.

    

    

      
3DReact Three Fiber + drei. drei's <Html transform occlude> mounts the React OS on the laptop screen, which is the same technique as this prototype. The model is made in Blender with baked lighting and exported as a Draco GLB under 1.5 MB.

      
Scroll + motionGSAP ScrollTrigger and Lenis (both already installed) drive the camera dolly. Reduced motion skips the dolly and opens the OS directly.

      
Content + wallOne content.js (projects, roles, skills) feeds the OS windows, the terminal, and a plain HTML résumé below the scene for SEO and recruiters. The Visitor Wall uses a Supabase table with a pending/approved flag. This prototype only mocks it in the browser.
